What the promoted posts returned, and where the next dollar could go

Like rate = likes ÷ views, the one engagement signal every platform reports the same way.

  1. Split the budget by job. Most of it behind song material, which is what converts to streams: performance clips like “He Is Worthy of Praise” (5.6%), the 30-second lyric cuts, the trailers. A smaller share, 10–20%, behind the stickiest story pieces (the crocodile skit, Ep. 1 and Ep. 2) for cheap attention and a warm audience to retarget. Boost the original posts (Spark Ads on TikTok) so the comments and likes travel with them: TikTok’s own benchmark puts Spark Ads at 142% higher engagement and 30% lower cost per click than standard in-feed ads.
  2. Buy views and streams rather than followers. A large follower number has its uses, but the follower campaigns are not building an audience that watches: 0.0% of August TikTok views came from the Following feed, and a new post reaches under 200 of the 87K followers. If the number itself is the goal, keep it separate from the budget meant to move views and streams. For streams, a conversion objective on a pixel-tracked song page returns about 2.4× more Spotify saves per dollar than a click campaign.
  3. Set the countries. Left worldwide, Meta and TikTok spend where reach is cheapest, which is why Syria, Nigeria and the Philippines lead the audience. Start with United States, Canada, United Kingdom and Australia; add Brazil and the Philippines as their own small ad sets, since those listeners do engage.
  4. Retarget the watchers. Serve the lyric cut to people who watched half of a story piece or the trailer. Warm audiences stream at roughly a fifth of the cost of cold ones.
  5. Test small, scale the winner. $5–10 a day per creative for a week; scale the one with the best like-and-save rate, no more than 20% a day so the platform’s learning does not reset.

How we know this. The numbers are read straight from the Fitz dashboards and the public YouTube channel. The benchmarks are the ones labels and publishers pass around: MusicPulse’s 2026 round-up of TikTok, Chartmetric and Feature.fm data, Hypebot on Spark Ads and Adligator’s CPM by country. The rest is our own experience running campaigns for music releases, where the same thing keeps showing up: posts people already chose to watch travel further per dollar than announcements.
